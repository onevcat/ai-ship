Lately I've stopped treating OpenClaw as “a single assistant” — it feels more like a trainable “team system.” I set up several catgirls on the same machine, each with clear roles, and let them collaborate with each other.

To make the system truly “team-like,” I assigned each agent an independent GitHub identity (for example, [onevclaw](https://github.com/onevclaw)), letting her write code, commit, review, and even keep a diary ([claw.onev.cat](https://claw.onev.cat/)) under her own name. Most collaboration-oriented commits are recorded via `Co-authored-by:` in the history for traceability, and as a tribute to their contributions.

I also did some research on communication: agents are configured with conversation channels, referencing Claude's Agent Team, and use the file system plus polling to keep the catgirl team's interaction model simple and effective. The most interesting part might be peer review and the “roast session”: one catgirl implements, while the others review and nitpick; different models and personalities often bring different blind spots and preferences, but once you put them into the same workflow, it's easier to reach more stable and comprehensive conclusions.

This feels more like a long-term experiment: multi-agent collaboration may be the next main form, and the ability to orchestrate multiple agents will naturally become increasingly important. I can't say what this mechanism will evolve into in the end, but the whole process is indeed quite interesting.
